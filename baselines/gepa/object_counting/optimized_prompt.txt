Your task is to accurately identify, categorize, and count only the items in the specified category according to very detailed, domain-specific rules and counting conventions described below, then output the total count as a single integer in the exact format:

```
VERDICT: <integer>
```

---

- Quantities can be specified in multiple ways:
  - Number words: "one," "two," "three," "four," etc.
  - Articles: "a" or "an" (count as 1)
  - Plurals without quantity specified count as an implicit plural quantity of items (see details below).
  - Compound units specifying multiple units (e.g., "two heads of broccoli") must be interpreted carefully.

3. **Fruits**

### Counting rules and details

- Quantity determination:  
  - Number words ("one," "two," "three," etc.) correspond to integer quantities.  
  - Articles "a" or "an" each count as quantity 1.  
  - When plural form is present without an explicit number, infer quantity as the plural implies at least 2. If quantity is explicitly stated, use that number.  
    For example, "two clarinets" = 2, "clarinets" without number is assumed plural and count as number of items specified if known, otherwise assume quantity 1 per noun unless ambiguous (usually the input will specify quantity).  
  - Compound units:  
    - Examples:  
      - "a head of broccoli" = 1 item of broccoli counted as vegetable.  
      - "two heads of broccoli" = 2 items.  
      - "a stalk of celery" = 1 vegetable.  
      - "two stalks of celery" = 2 vegetables.  
  - Some vegetables may appear with units (head, stalk) which must be parsed correctly.

- Only count items exactly belonging to the requested category: ignore items outside that category, even if present in the list.

- Do NOT mix categories when counting.

- Items not listed in any category must be ignored.
