**Comprehensive SVG Path Guide for Advanced Use Cases**

---

### 1. **Combining Subpaths and Calculating Polygon Edges**

- **Each `M` command signals a new subpath.**
- **To analyze the full shape, integrate all connected subpaths into one closed polygon.**
- **Count every unique vertex linked via line commands (`L`) within the merged subpaths that form this closed polygon.**
- **Ensure closure by confirming the last vertex reconnects to the polygon’s first vertex.**
- **The total count of distinct vertices after merging equals the polygon’s edge count.**

---

### 2. **Determining Polygon Types from Vertices and Edges**

- After merging subpaths as above, tally all unique points connected by `L` commands.
- A polygon is closed if the path ends exactly where it started.
- Polygon classification by edges:
  - 3 edges → Triangle
  - 4 edges → Quadrilateral (kite, rectangle, trapezoid)
  - 5 edges → Pentagon
  - 6 edges → Hexagon
  - 7 edges → Heptagon
  - 8 edges → Octagon

---

### 3. **Classifying Quadrilaterals**

- **Kite:** Has two pairs of adjacent sides equal in length.
- **Rectangle:** Opposite sides are parallel and equal, with all interior angles at 90°.
- **Trapezoid:** Contains at least one pair of parallel sides but with unequal adjacent side lengths.

*Use coordinate data to calculate side lengths and verify parallelism to distinguish these forms.*

---

### 4. **Understanding Arc Commands (`A`)**

- Syntax: `A rx ry x-axis-rotation large-arc-flag sweep-flag x y`
- Two arcs sharing identical radii and rotation that connect seamlessly create an ellipse.
- A circle is a special ellipse where radii are equal (`rx = ry`) and rotation is zero.
- A closed shape made from a single arc plus two straight lines represents a sector (pie slice).

---

### 5. **Line Segments**

- A path with only two points connected by a single `L` command and no closure represents a line segment.

---

### 6. **Summary Table**

| Shape        | SVG Path Characteristics                                                        | How to Identify                                                                                 |
|--------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Line**     | Two points, one `L` command, path open                                        | Exactly two points                                                                              |
| **Triangle** | Three points, closed path                                                      | Three unique vertices after merging, path returns to start                                    |
| **Kite**     | Four points, two pairs of adjacent equal-length sides                         | Adjacent sides have equal lengths                                                             |
| **Rectangle**| Four points, opposite sides equal and parallel, all angles 90°                | Parallel opposite sides, equal length, all right angles                                       |
| **Trapezoid**| Four points, at least one pair of parallel sides, unequal adjacent sides       | Detect one pair of parallel sides with differing side lengths                                 |
| **Pentagon** | Five points, closed path                                                       | Five unique vertices after merging subpaths                                                   |
| **Hexagon**  | Six points, closed path                                                        | Six unique vertices after merging subpaths                                                    |
| **Heptagon** | Seven points, closed path                                                      | Seven unique vertices after merging subpaths                                                  |
| **Octagon**  | Eight points, closed path                                                      | Eight unique vertices after merging subpaths                                                  |
| **Sector**   | One arc plus two lines forming a closed shape                                 | Single `A` command combined with two `L` commands creating a pie slice                         |
| **Ellipse**  | Two arcs with matching `rx`, `ry`, and rotation, forming a closed loop        | Two arcs share identical radii and rotation, path closes                                     |
| **Circle**   | Ellipse with equal radii and zero rotation                                   | Same as ellipse but with no rotation                                                          |

---

### 7. **Calculating Side Lengths**

For two points \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\):

\[
\text{length} = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
\]

Use this to measure side lengths to differentiate kites, rectangles, and trapezoids.

---

### 8. **Identifying Figures Defined by Arcs**

- Two arcs with matching radii and rotation forming a continuous loop indicate an ellipse or circle.
- A single arc plus two line segments forming a closed path indicate a sector.

---

**Reminder:** Always combine all subpaths that begin with `M` commands into a single continuous shape before counting vertices and edges. This ensures accurate side counts and correct shape classification.
