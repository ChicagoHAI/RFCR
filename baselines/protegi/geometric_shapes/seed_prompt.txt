**SVG Path Cheat Sheet for Difficult Examples**

---

### 1. **Identifying Polygons by Counting Points and Sides**

- **Count unique points connected by lines (L commands).**
- **Closed shape:** Path returns to starting point.
- **Number of sides = number of unique points.**
- **Polygon names:**
  - 3 sides → Triangle
  - 4 sides → Quadrilateral (kite, rectangle, trapezoid)
  - 5 sides → Pentagon
  - 6 sides → Hexagon
  - 7 sides → Heptagon
  - 8 sides → Octagon

---

### 2. **Distinguishing Quadrilaterals (4-sided shapes)**

- **Kite:**
  - Two pairs of adjacent sides equal.
  - Often formed by points where adjacent side lengths match.
  - Check side lengths for pairs of adjacent equal lengths.
- **Rectangle:**
  - Opposite sides equal and parallel.
  - All angles 90°.
  - Coordinates show vertical and horizontal lines with equal lengths.
- **Trapezoid:**
  - At least one pair of parallel sides.
  - Sides may not be equal.
  - Usually top and bottom sides have similar y-values (horizontal), but lengths differ.
  - Sides adjacent to parallel sides are not parallel.

**Tip:** When unsure, calculate distances between points to check side lengths and identify pairs of equal sides.

---

### 3. **Interpreting Arc Commands (`A`)**

- **Format:** `A rx, ry x-axis-rotation large-arc-flag sweep-flag x,y`
- **Arcs with equal rx and ry and two arcs forming a closed path → Ellipse**
  - If rotation is 0 and rx=ry → Circle (special case of ellipse).
  - If rotation ≠ 0 → Ellipse.
- **Arc combined with two straight lines forming a closed shape → Sector**
  - Shape looks like a "pie slice" with two radii and an arc.
- **Multiple arcs with equal radii and rotation forming closed shape → Ellipse**
- **If path contains arcs and lines forming a closed shape with two straight sides and one curved side → Sector**

---

### 4. **Handling Multiple Subpaths (Multiple `M` commands)**

- **Multiple `M` commands split the path into subpaths.**
- **To identify the shape:**
  - Consider all connected points in order.
  - Count unique points connected by lines.
  - Check if the path forms a closed polygon by returning to the starting point.
- **If subpaths connect to form a closed polygon, count all unique points for polygon sides.**

---

### 5. **Lines**

- **If path has only two points connected by a single `L` command → Line**
- **No closed shape or multiple points → Line**

---

### 6. **Summary Table**

| Shape     | Key Features in SVG Path                                                                                  | Identification Tips                                                                                   |
|-----------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Line** | Only two points connected by one `L` command, no closure                                                | Two points only                                                                                      |
| **Triangle** | Three points connected, path closed (returns to start)                                                | Count 3 unique points, closed path                                                                  |
| **Kite** | Four points, two pairs of adjacent sides equal                                                          | Check side lengths for adjacent equal pairs                                                        |
| **Rectangle** | Four points, opposite sides equal and parallel, all angles 90°                                       | Coordinates show vertical/horizontal lines, equal opposite sides                                    |
| **Trapezoid** | Four points, at least one pair of parallel sides, sides not necessarily equal                       | Check for one pair of parallel sides, side lengths differ                                          |
| **Pentagon** | Five points, closed path                                                                              | Count 5 unique points                                                                                |
| **Hexagon** | Six points, closed path                                                                                 | Count 6 unique points                                                                                |
| **Heptagon** | Seven points, closed path                                                                              | Count 7 unique points                                                                                |
| **Octagon** | Eight points, closed path                                                                               | Count 8 unique points                                                                                |
| **Sector** | Path with two straight lines and one arc forming a closed shape                                         | Look for `A` command with two `L` commands forming a pie-slice shape                                |
| **Ellipse** | Path with two `A` commands with equal rx and ry, possibly rotated, forming a closed shape              | Two arcs with equal radii and rotation, closed path                                                |
| **Circle** | Special case of ellipse with rx=ry and rotation=0                                                      | Same as ellipse but no rotation                                                                     |

---

### 7. **How to Calculate Side Lengths**

Given two points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2):

\[
\text{distance} = \sqrt{(x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2}
\]

Use this to check for equal sides (adjacent or opposite) to distinguish kites, rectangles, and trapezoids.

---

### 8. **Recognizing Shapes with Arcs**

- **Two arcs with same radii and rotation, connecting start and end points → Ellipse**
- **Arc plus two lines forming a closed shape → Sector**
- **Arc with large-arc-flag and sweep-flag parameters define the arc's sweep and size**

---

### 9. **Tips for Quick Identification**

- **Count points and check closure for polygons.**
- **Check for arcs (`A` commands) to identify sectors or ellipses.**
- **For quadrilaterals, calculate side lengths to distinguish kite, rectangle, trapezoid.**
- **Multiple `M` commands indicate subpaths; consider all connected points for polygon sides.**
- **If only two points and one line → line.**

Question: This SVG path element <path d="M 55.58,17.52 L 53.95,26.14 L 47.22,29.95 M 47.22,29.95 L 48.21,22.28 L 55.58,17.52"/> draws a
Options:
(A) circle
(B) heptagon
(C) hexagon
(D) kite
(E) line
(F) octagon
(G) pentagon
(H) rectangle
(I) sector
(J) triangle
Reasoning: Let's think step by step.
This SVG path element contains "M" and "L" commands. "M" moves the current point to the specified coordinates, and "L" draws a line to the next point. The path is split into two subpaths by the second "M".

First subpath:
(1) M 55.58,17.52: Move to 55.58,17.52.
(2) L 53.95,26.14: Line to 53.95,26.14.
(3) L 47.22,29.95: Line to 47.22,29.95.

Second subpath:
(4) M 47.22,29.95: Move to 47.22,29.95.
(5) L 48.21,22.28: Line to 48.21,22.28.
(6) L 55.58,17.52: Line to 55.58,17.52.

If we connect the points in order, the lines form a closed four-sided shape: 55.58,17.52 → 53.95,26.14 → 47.22,29.95 → 48.21,22.28 → 55.58,17.52. This is a quadrilateral. Among the four-sided options, "kite" and "rectangle" are possible. The coordinates suggest two pairs of adjacent sides are equal in length, which is characteristic of a kite, not a rectangle. So the answer is (D).
Answer: (D)

(1) M 72.96,32.63: Move to 72.96,32.63.  
(2) L 7.85,57.83: Draw a line to 7.85,57.83.  
(3) M 7.85,57.83: Move to 7.85,57.83 (this is the same as the previous point, so it continues from here).
