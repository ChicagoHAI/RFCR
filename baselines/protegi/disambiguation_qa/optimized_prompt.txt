**Pronoun Antecedent Resolution Cheat Sheet for Difficult/Ambiguous Cases**

---

### 1. **Ambiguity Due to Both Referents Being Plausible**

- When both possible antecedents can logically perform the action or possess the attribute described by the pronoun, **the pronoun is ambiguous**.
- Examples:
  - *"Alex did not know Taylor until he sent a letter."*  
    Both Alex and Taylor could have sent the letter → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The developer argued with the designer because he liked the design."*  
    Both could like the design → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The scientist collaborates with the artist, and she shares a story."*  
    Either could share the story → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The mechanic greets the receptionist because he was in a good mood."*  
    Either could be in a good mood → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The nurse stayed away from the laborer because she was very busy."*  
    Either could be busy → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The developer met with the secretary because she made a mistake."*  
    Either could have made a mistake → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The reporter and the chef discuss his favorite dishes."*  
    Could refer to either → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The developer met with the designer because their idea can be implemented."*  
    Could be developer’s or designer’s idea → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The reporter and the chef will discuss his favorite dishes."*  
    Could refer to either → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The scientist collaborates with the artist, and they share a story."*  
    "They" could mean both or either → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The developer was unable to communicate with the writer because they used big words."*  
    Both could have used big words → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The mechanic disliked the cashier because she is arrogant."*  
    Both could be arrogant → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The mechanic greets the receptionist because she was in a good mood."*  
    Both could be in a good mood → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The mechanic greets the receptionist because he is standing in front of the door."*  
    Both could be standing there → **Ambiguous**
  - *"The developer was unable to communicate with the writer because she used big words."*  
    Both could have used big words → **Ambiguous**

---

### 2. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Being Informed or Advised**

- When the sentence involves someone **telling, informing, advising, warning, or giving feedback**, the pronoun usually refers to the **recipient** of the information or advice, not the speaker.
- Examples:
  - *"The technician told the customer that she could pay with cash."* → "she" = customer
  - *"The hygienist told the patient that she should floss every day."* → "she" = patient
  - *"The engineer informed the client that they would need to make all future payments on time."* → "they" = client
  - *"The surgeon warned the patient that she needed to get more rest."* → "she" = patient
  - *"The physician told the baker that she had cancer."* → "she" = baker
  - *"The supervisor gave the employee feedback on their stellar performance."* → "their" = employee
  - *"The supervisor gave the employee feedback on her stellar performance."* → "her" = employee
  - *"The scientist told the baker that they had cancer."* → "they" = baker
  - *"The hygienist told the patient that he should floss every day."* → "he" = patient
  - *"The physician told the baker that he had cancer."* → "he" = baker
  - *"The technician told the customer that he had completed the repair."* → "he" = technician
  - *"The technician told the customer that she had completed the repair."* → "she" = technician

---

### 3. **Pronouns Referring to the Subject Who Initiates an Action**

- When the subject performs an action and then refers to a pronoun related to that action, the pronoun often refers to the subject, especially if the object is unlikely to be the referent.
- Examples:
  - *"The carpenter told the onlooker that they had been working on the house for three weeks."* → "they" = carpenter
  - *"The carpenter told the onlooker that he had been working on the house for three weeks."* → "he" = carpenter
  - *"The janitor warned the child not to step on the wet floor, otherwise he would have to mop it all over again."* → "he" = janitor
  - *"The janitor warned the child not to step on the wet floor, otherwise she would have to mop it all over again."* → "she" = janitor
  - *"The carpenter collaborated with the designer and gave them a prototype."* → "them" = designer
  - *"The designer collaborated with the carpenter and gave them a blueprint."* → "them" = carpenter
  - *"The carpenter collaborated with the designer and gave him a prototype."* → "him" = designer
  - *"The designer collaborated with the carpenter and gave him a blueprint."* → "him" = carpenter
  - *"The cleaner called the guard and asked them to open the door."* → "them" = guard
  - *"The cleaner called the guard and asked her to open the door."* → "her" = guard
  - *"The guard called the cleaner and asked her to open the door."* → "her" = cleaner
  - *"The designer called the janitor and asked her to clean the room."* → "her" = janitor
  - *"The carpenter made a desk for the clerk and gave them a discount."* → "them" = clerk
  - *"The chief thanked the housekeeper and gave her some tips."* → "her" = housekeeper

---

### 4. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Needs or Requests Something**

- When the sentence involves someone **requesting, asking for, or needing** something, the pronoun usually refers to the person who needs or requests it.
- Examples:
  - *"The laborer handed the secretary a hammer because they asked for a tool."* → "they" = secretary
  - *"The assistant asked the physician if she could borrow some money."* → "she" = assistant
  - *"The assistant asked the physician if he needs any help."* → "he" = physician
  - *"The assistant asked the physician if they could borrow some money."* → "they" = assistant
  - *"The assistant asked the physician if they need any help."* → "they" = physician
  - *"The taxpayer met with the accountant to find out if they could help with tax preparation."* → "they" = accountant

---

### 5. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Likes, Dislikes, or Has an Opinion**

- When the sentence involves **liking, disliking, or expressing an opinion**, the pronoun can be ambiguous if both parties could logically have that feeling.
- Examples:
  - *"The mechanic disliked the cashier because she is arrogant."* → ambiguous (could be either)
  - *"The mechanic disliked the cashier because he is arrogant."* → ambiguous (could be either)
  - *"The developer argued with the designer because he liked the design."* → ambiguous
  - *"The mechanic greets the receptionist because he was in a good mood."* → ambiguous
  - *"The mechanic greets the receptionist because she was in a good mood."* → ambiguous
  - *"The visitor told the teacher that he liked the cake."* → "he" = visitor (more natural)
  - *"The visitor told the teacher that she liked the cake."* → ambiguous if both female

---

### 6. **Pronouns Referring to Offices or Locations**

- When a pronoun refers to an office or location associated with a person, ambiguity arises if both parties could own the office.
- Examples:
  - *"Bailey planned to meet the director at their office."* → ambiguous (could be either)
  - *"Bailey planned to meet the director at his office."* → ambiguous (could be either)
  - *"Bailey planned to meet the director at her office."* → ambiguous (could be either)
  - *"After meeting with the producers, Sam goes to his office."* → "his" = Sam (more natural)
  - *"After meeting with the producers, Sam goes to their office."* → ambiguous
  - *"After meeting with the producers, Sam went to their office."* → ambiguous

---

### 7. **Pronouns Referring to Groups or Collective Entities**

- When pronouns like "they" or "their" refer to groups or collective nouns, ambiguity can arise if the group includes multiple people.
- Examples:
  - *"This story could not be published without the writer and their friends' support."* → "their" = writer (most natural)
  - *"The reporter and the chef will discuss their favorite dishes."* → ambiguous (could be either or both)
  - *"The scientist collaborates with the artist, and they share a story."* → ambiguous (could be both or either)

---

### 8. **Pronouns Referring to People with Different Genders or Numbers**

- Pronouns must agree in number and gender with their antecedents.
- Singular pronouns (he, she, his, her) usually refer to singular antecedents.
- Plural pronouns (they, their, them) can refer to singular antecedents when used as singular "they," but ambiguity can arise.
- Examples:
  - *"Alex tells us that he could not meet."* → "he" = Alex (singular vs. plural "us")
  - *"Alex tells us that they could not meet."* → ambiguous if "they" is singular or plural
  - *"The mechanic called to inform the customer that his car would be ready."* → "his" = customer (more natural)
  - *"The mechanic called to inform the customer that her car would be ready."* → "her" = customer (more natural)

---

### 9. **Pronouns Referring to People in Context of Roles or Expertise**

- When the sentence involves roles or expertise, the pronoun usually refers to the person logically associated with that role.
- Examples:
  - *"The patient was referred to the specialist because he is an expert on rare skin conditions."* → "he" = specialist
  - *"The customer and the chef discuss his culinary training."* → "his" = chef (more logical)
  - *"The customer and the chef discuss her culinary training."* → "her" = chef (more logical)
  - *"The educator was meeting with a student to discuss his grading policy."* → "his" = educator (more logical)
  - *"The educator was meeting with a student to discuss their grading policy."* → "their" = educator (more logical)

---

### 10. **Pronouns Referring to People in Context of Actions or Conditions**

- When the sentence involves actions or conditions (e.g., illness, busyness), the pronoun can be ambiguous if both parties could logically be the referent.
- Examples:
  - *"The nurse stayed away from the laborer because she was very busy."* → ambiguous
  - *"The nurse was running from the laborer because she smelled awful."* → ambiguous
  - *"The nurse was running from the laborer because he smelled awful."* → ambiguous
  - *"The surgeon warned the nurse that she needed more time to prepare."* → ambiguous
  - *"The surgeon warned the patient that they needed to get more rest."* → "they" = patient (more natural)
  - *"The janitor warned the child not to step on the wet floor, otherwise she would have to mop it all over again."* → "she" = janitor (more natural)

---

### Summary of Key Strategies:

- **Check the verb and context:** Who is more likely to perform the action or possess the attribute?
- **Consider roles and typical scenarios:** E.g., doctors inform patients, supervisors give feedback to employees.
- **Look for logical consistency:** Does it make sense for the pronoun to refer to the subject or object?
- **Number and gender agreement:** Pronouns usually match the number and gender of their antecedents.
- **If both interpretations are plausible and no context clarifies, mark as ambiguous.**

Question: In the following sentences, explain the antecedent of the pronoun (which thing the pronoun refers to), or state that it is ambiguous.
Sentence: The technician told the customer that they could pay with cash.
Options:
(A) The technician could pay
(B) The customer could pay
(C) Ambiguous
Reasoning: Let's think step by step.  
Here we need to determine who the pronoun "they" refers to. There are two possible referents: the technician and the customer. The verb "told" and the phrase "could pay with cash" might help us decide. Let X be the technician and Y the customer. The sentence is of the form "X told Y that (X or Y) could pay with cash."

Let's consider Y first: "X told Y that Y could pay with cash." This makes sense, because the technician is likely informing the customer about their payment options.

Now, consider X: "X told Y that X could pay with cash." This is less likely, because it would be odd for the technician to tell the customer about the technician's own ability to pay.

Therefore, the pronoun "they" most likely refers to the customer. So the answer is (B).
Answer: (B)

Sentence: Alex did not know Taylor until she sent a letter.

(A) Alex sent the letter
(B) Taylor sent the letter
(C) Ambiguous
Reasoning: Let's think step by step.  
Here we need to determine who the pronoun "she" refers to. There are two possible referents for "she": Alex and Taylor. The sentence is of the form "X did not know Y until she sent a letter."  
Let's consider Y first: "X did not know Y until Y sent a letter." This makes sense, as Y could have initiated contact by sending a letter, leading to X knowing Y.  
Now, consider X: "X did not know Y until X sent a letter." This also makes sense, as X could have sent a letter to Y, which could have led to them getting to know each other.  
Because both interpretations are possible and the sentence does not provide enough information to determine which one is correct, the antecedent of "she" is ambiguous. So the answer is (C).
Answer: (C)
