**Cheat Sheet for Counting Objects, Vegetables, Fruits, Animals, and Musical Instruments**

---

### General Counting Rules:
- Count each item individually.
- For plural quantities (e.g., "three apples"), add the number specified.
- For singular items, count as 1.
- Only count items that belong to the specified category (objects, vegetables, fruits, animals, musical instruments).

---

### Categories & Key Details

#### 1. **Objects**
- Includes: couch, bed, table, lamp, car, chair, toaster, stove, fridge, oven, microwave.
- Count all items regardless of type.
- Example: "three couches, a bed, a table, and a lamp" → 3 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 6 objects.

---

#### 2. **Vegetables**
- Common vegetables to count:
  - Cabbage, carrot, stalk of celery, onion, garlic, cauliflower, broccoli (head or heads), lettuce (head or heads), potato, yam.
- Plurals and multiple heads count individually.
- Exclude non-vegetables (e.g., fruits, animals, musical instruments).
- Examples:
  - "two heads of broccoli" = 2 vegetables.
  - "five carrots" = 5 vegetables.
  - "a stalk of celery" = 1 vegetable.
- Watch for mixed items: only count vegetables.
- Some tricky items:
  - Garlic: count each garlic (plural "garlics" means multiple).
  - Lettuce head: count each head.
  - Broccoli: count each head.
- Do NOT count fruits or animals mistakenly as vegetables.

---

#### 3. **Fruits**
- Common fruits to count:
  - Apple, banana, orange, peach, plum, nectarine, raspberry, blackberry, strawberry, grape, blueberry (if mentioned).
- Plurals and multiple quantities count individually.
- Examples:
  - "two apples" = 2 fruits.
  - "three raspberries" = 3 fruits.
- Exclude vegetables, animals, and other categories.
- Watch for mixed lists: only count fruits.
- Some tricky points:
  - Bananas, grapes, and berries count individually.
  - "Head of broccoli" is NOT a fruit.
  - "Garlic" is NOT a fruit.

---

#### 4. **Animals**
- Common animals to count:
  - Mouse, snail, chicken, cat, pig, snake, fish, goat, bear, dog, cow, frog, donkey, rabbit, duck.
- Plurals and multiple quantities count individually.
- Examples:
  - "three pigs" = 3 animals.
  - "a dog" = 1 animal.
- Exclude fruits, vegetables, and objects.
- Watch for mixed lists: only count animals.
- Some tricky points:
  - Snails and frogs are animals.
  - Fish count as animals.
  - Do NOT count musical instruments or objects.

---

#### 5. **Musical Instruments**
- Common instruments to count:
  - Flute, trombone, accordion, clarinet, drum, piano, violin, trumpet.
- Plurals and multiple quantities count individually.
- Examples:
  - "three drums" = 3 instruments.
  - "a flute and a trombone" = 2 instruments.
- Exclude animals, fruits, vegetables, and objects.
- Watch for mixed lists: only count musical instruments.

---

### Tips for Difficult Examples

- **Mixed categories in one list:** Identify the category asked, then count only those items.
- **Plural forms:** Always multiply by the number given.
- **Compound items:** "head of broccoli" counts as 1 vegetable; "two heads of broccoli" counts as 2.
- **Ambiguous items:** 
  - "Garlic" is a vegetable.
  - "Lettuce head" is a vegetable.
  - "Stalk of celery" is a vegetable.
  - "Snail" is an animal.
  - "Accordion" is a musical instrument.
- **Avoid counting items outside the category.** For example, when counting fruits, do not count vegetables or animals.
- **When counting objects, include all household items listed.**

### Example Walkthrough

**Example:**  
"I have a stalk of celery, two drums, two onions, a carrot, an accordion, a yam, a cabbage, a lettuce head, a potato, and a head of broccoli. How many vegetables do I have?"  
- Vegetables: stalk of celery (1), two onions (2), carrot (1), yam (1), cabbage (1), lettuce head (1), potato (1), head of broccoli (1)  
- Total vegetables = 1 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 9
