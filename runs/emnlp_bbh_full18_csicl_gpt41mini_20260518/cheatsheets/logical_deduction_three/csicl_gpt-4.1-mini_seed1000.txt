**Cheat Sheet for Solving Ordering Logic Problems with Three Objects**

---

### 1. **Basic Setup**

- You have three objects (e.g., birds, books, fruits, vehicles, golfers).
- Statements describe relative orderings (left/right, older/newer, more/less expensive, finished above/below).
- Goal: Determine the exact order or position of one object (e.g., leftmost, second from left, oldest, second-most expensive).

---

### 2. **Key Concepts**

- **Relative Positioning**:  
  - "A is to the left of B" means A is somewhere left of B (not necessarily immediately adjacent).  
  - "A is older than B" means A ranks higher (older) than B.  
  - "A finished above B" means A ranks better (higher) than B.  
  - "A is the second from the left" means A is in the middle position.

- **Absolute Positioning**:  
  - "A is the leftmost" means A is in position 1 (far left).  
  - "A is the rightmost" means A is in position 3 (far right).  
  - "A is the second from the left" means A is in position 2 (middle).

- **Combining Statements**:  
  Use all given statements to deduce a consistent linear order of the three objects.

---

### 3. **Common Difficult Patterns & How to Handle Them**

#### A. **When given two relative orderings that form a chain**

Example:  
- "Quail is to the left of Owl."  
- "Owl is to the left of Hummingbird."

**Approach:**  
- Chain the order: Quail < Owl < Hummingbird.  
- Positions: Quail (1), Owl (2), Hummingbird (3).

---

#### B. **When one object’s position is explicitly stated (e.g., second from left)**

Example:  
- "Raven is the second from the left."  
- "Blue jay is to the right of Hummingbird."

**Approach:**  
- Place the explicitly positioned object in the middle (position 2).  
- Use relative order to place the others around it.

---

#### C. **When statements involve "older/newer" or "more/less expensive"**

- "Older" or "more expensive" means higher rank (leftmost or first).  
- "Newer" or "less expensive" means lower rank (rightmost or last).

Example:  
- "Limousine is older than Bus."  
- "Bus is older than Convertible."

**Approach:**  
- Limousine > Bus > Convertible (Limousine oldest, Convertible newest).

---

#### D. **When the "second-newest" or "second-most expensive" is given**

Example:  
- "Minivan is second-newest."  
- "Station wagon is newer than Minivan."

**Approach:**  
- Second-newest = middle position (2).  
- Station wagon is newer → position 1 (newest).  
- Minivan = 2, so the remaining vehicle is oldest (3).

---

#### E. **When contradictory or overlapping statements appear**

Example:  
- "Sedan is older than Tractor."  
- "Bus is older than Sedan."  
- Question: Who is oldest?

**Approach:**  
- Chain: Bus > Sedan > Tractor.  
- Oldest is Bus.

---

#### F. **When multiple relative positions are given but no explicit absolute position**

Example:  
- "Crow is to the left of Owl."  
- "Owl is second from the left."

**Approach:**  
- Owl = position 2.  
- Crow is left of Owl → Crow = position 1.  
- Remaining object = position 3.

---

### 4. **Step-by-Step Method**

1. **Identify all relative orderings** (e.g., A left of B, A older than B).  
2. **Identify any absolute positions** (e.g., A is second from left).  
3. **Place absolute positions first** (if any).  
4. **Use relative orderings to place other objects around absolute positions.**  
5. **Check for consistency and adjust if needed.**  
6. **Answer the question based on the final order.**

---

### 5. **Special Notes**

- "Finished below" means worse rank (to the right or lower).  
- "Second from the left" = middle position (2).  
- "Second-newest" or "second-most expensive" = middle position (2).  
- When "X is to the right of Y" and "Z is the second from the left," use these to deduce the order:  
  - Place the second from left object in position 2.  
  - Use "to the right/left" to place others accordingly.

---

### 6. **Examples of Applying the Cheat Sheet**

- **Example 1:**  
  "Quail is to the left of Owl."  
  "Owl is to the left of Hummingbird."  
  → Order: Quail (1), Owl (2), Hummingbird (3).

- **Example 2:**  
  "Blue jay is to the right of Hummingbird."  
  "Raven is second from the left."  
  → Raven = 2, Hummingbird left of Blue jay, so Hummingbird = 1, Blue jay = 3.

- **Example 3:**  
  "Minivan is second-newest."  
  "Station wagon is newer than Minivan."  
  → Station wagon = 1 (newest), Minivan = 2, Truck = 3 (oldest).

---

### 7. **Avoid Common Mistakes**

- Do not confuse "older" with "newer" or "more expensive" with "cheaper."  
- "Second from the left" is always position 2, not 1 or 3.  
- When given "X is the second from the left" and "Y is to the left of X," Y must be position 1.  
- When given "X is the second from the left" and "Y is to the right of X," Y must be position 3.

Question: The following paragraphs each describe a set of three objects arranged in a fixed order. The statements are logically consistent within each paragraph. On a branch, there are three birds: a quail, an owl, and a hummingbird. The quail is to the left of the owl. The owl is to the left of the hummingbird.
Options:
(A) The quail is the leftmost
(B) The owl is the leftmost
(C) The hummingbird is the leftmost
Reasoning: Let's think step by step. (1) The white book is to the left of the gray book: "(left) ? white ? gray ? (right)". (2) The red book is the second from the left: "(left) ? white red gray ? (right)". (3) Combining (1) and (2) we get the following ordering: "(left) white red gray (right)". According to this ordering, the leftmost book is the white book. The white book is the leftmost. So the answer is (C).
Answer: (A)
