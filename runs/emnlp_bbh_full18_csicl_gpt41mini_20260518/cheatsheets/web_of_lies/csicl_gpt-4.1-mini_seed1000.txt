Cheat Sheet for Solving Truth/Lie Chain Problems (Focus on Difficult Cases)

---

### Core Principles

1. **Initial Known Fact**: Start with the given fact about the first person (truth-teller or liar). This is your anchor.

2. **Evaluating Claims**:
   - If a person **tells the truth**, their statement about another person’s truthfulness or lying is **correct**.
   - If a person **lies**, their statement about another person’s truthfulness or lying is **incorrect**.

3. **Chain Reasoning**:
   - Move step-by-step through the chain of claims.
   - At each step, use the previous person's truth status to evaluate the current claim.
   - Update the current person's truth status accordingly.

---

### Handling Contradictory or Complex Chains

- **When a person says another "lies" or "tells the truth"**:
  - If the speaker is truthful, the claim matches the actual status of the person they talk about.
  - If the speaker lies, the claim is the opposite of the actual status.

- **Multiple layers of claims**:
  - Carefully track each person's truth status.
  - Use a table or list to keep track of each person’s status as you progress.
  - Re-check previous conclusions if contradictions arise.

---

### Specific Tips for Difficult Examples

1. **Long Chains with Alternating Truth and Lies**:
   - Alternate truth and lie statuses often appear.
   - Example pattern: Truth → Truth → Lie → Truth → Lie ...
   - Use the initial known fact to propagate truth/lie status down the chain.

2. **Claims About Claims**:
   - When a person says another "tells the truth" or "lies," and that person is also making a claim about a third person, carefully evaluate each link.
   - Example: A says B tells the truth; B says C lies; C says D tells the truth.
   - Evaluate A → B → C → D stepwise.

3. **When the Last Person Claims the Previous Person Lies**:
   - If the previous person is truthful, the last person’s claim is false → last person lies.
   - If the previous person lies, the last person’s claim is true → last person tells the truth.

4. **When the First Person is a Liar**:
   - The first claim about them is false.
   - This flips the interpretation of the first claim and affects the entire chain.

5. **When a Person Claims Another "Tells the Truth" but That Person is a Liar**:
   - The claim is false → the speaker lies.
   - This can cause a cascade of lies/truths down the chain.

6. **When a Person Claims Another "Lies" but That Person is Truthful**:
   - The claim is false → the speaker lies.
   - This is a key point to identify liars in the chain.

---

### Step-by-Step Method

1. **Identify the initial truth status** (given in the question).

2. **For each person in the chain**:
   - Read their claim about the previous person.
   - If the previous person’s status is known:
     - If the claim matches the previous person’s status, the current person tells the truth.
     - If the claim contradicts the previous person’s status, the current person lies.
   - If the previous person’s status is unknown, wait until it is determined.

3. **Continue until the last person’s status is determined**.

4. **Answer the question about the last person’s truthfulness**.

---

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Assuming all claims are true without checking the speaker’s status**.
- **Mixing up who is being talked about in the claim**.
- **Losing track of the chain order** — always follow the chain in the order given.
- **Forgetting to invert the claim if the speaker is a liar**.

---

### Example Mini-Template for Reasoning

- Given: Person A is truthful/liar.
- Person B says A tells the truth/lies.
- Evaluate B’s claim based on A’s status → B is truthful/liar.
- Person C says B tells the truth/lies.
- Evaluate C’s claim based on B’s status → C is truthful/liar.
- ...
- Continue until the last person.

---

### Summary

- Always start from the known fact.
- Evaluate each claim based on the speaker’s truth status.
- Use logical inversion when the speaker is a liar.
- Track each person’s status carefully.
- The last person’s truth status answers the question.

Reasoning: Let's think step by step.  
We are told that "Ryan tells the truth," so Ryan is a truth-teller. Next, "Helene says Ryan tells the truth" — since Ryan does tell the truth, Helene's claim is correct, so Helene tells the truth. Then, "Kandi says Helene tells the truth" — since Helene tells the truth, Kandi's claim is correct, so Kandi tells the truth. Next, "Inga says Kandi tells the truth" — since Kandi tells the truth, Inga's claim is correct, so Inga tells the truth. Finally, "Delbert says Inga lies" — since Inga tells the truth, Delbert's claim is incorrect, so Delbert lies. So the answer is No.
