Cheat Sheet for Solving Difficult Ordering Logic Problems

---

### 1. **General Approach to Ordering Problems**

- Identify the **five objects** and the **property/order** to be determined (e.g., age, price, finishing position, shelf position).
- Translate all given statements into **relative order relations** (e.g., A > B means A is newer/older/more expensive/faster than B).
- Use **fixed position clues** (e.g., "second from the left", "third-most expensive") as anchors.
- Combine inequalities and fixed positions to build a **complete or partial order**.
- Use process of elimination on options based on the constructed order.

---

### 2. **Handling Mixed Relative and Fixed Position Clues**

- When a statement says "X is the second-newest" or "Y is the third from the left," place that object in the exact position.
- Use inequalities to place other objects relative to the fixed one.
- Example: If Convertible is second-newest, then only one vehicle is newer; all others are older.

---

### 3. **Dealing with Conflicting Relative Statements**

- Sometimes statements give overlapping or indirect relations (e.g., "A is newer than B" and "B is older than C").
- Chain inequalities carefully: A > B and B > C implies A > C.
- Watch for **contradictions** or statements that confirm the order.

---

### 4. **Interpreting "Above" and "Below" in Rankings**

- "Finished above" means better rank (e.g., 1st is above 2nd).
- "Finished below" means worse rank.
- Use these to order players or objects in a tournament or race.

---

### 5. **Ordering by Price or Age**

- "Most expensive" = highest rank (1st).
- "Cheapest" = lowest rank (5th).
- "Second-cheapest" = 4th place.
- "Third-most expensive" = 3rd place.
- Use these to assign exact ranks when given.

---

### 6. **Bookshelf and Bird Positioning**

- "To the right of" means higher position number.
- "To the left of" means lower position number.
- Fixed positions like "second from the right" = position 4 (if 5 total).
- Use these to place objects on a line.

---

### 7. **Key Difficult Patterns and How to Solve Them**

#### A. **Antique Car Ordering with Mixed Age Clues**

- When given multiple relative age clues and fixed positions (e.g., "tractor is newer than minivan," "convertible is second-newest"), start by placing fixed positions.
- Use inequalities to place others.
- Example: If convertible is second-newest, only one vehicle is newer.
- If tractor is newer than minivan but older than limousine, order is: minivan < tractor < limousine.
- Combine with convertible and sedan relations to finalize.

#### B. **Golf Tournament Ranking with Multiple Relative Positions**

- Use "finished above/below" to create chains.
- Use fixed positions (e.g., "Joe finished last," "Ana finished second") as anchors.
- If "Eve finished below Amy" and "Mya finished second-to-last," place Mya at 4th, Joe at 5th, etc.
- Check for contradictions and adjust.

#### C. **Fruit Price Ordering with Mixed Clues**

- Translate "most expensive," "second-cheapest," "third-most expensive" into ranks 1 to 5.
- Use inequalities to order fruits.
- Example: If watermelons are most expensive (1), plums are second-cheapest (4), pears are third-most expensive (3), then loquats and peaches fill remaining ranks.
- Use "more expensive than" and "less expensive than" to confirm.

#### D. **Bookshelf Ordering with Relative and Fixed Positions**

- Use "to the right/left of" to order books.
- Use fixed positions like "second from the right" to place books.
- Example: If blue book is second from right (position 4), purple book is second from left (position 2), and blue is right of yellow, place yellow accordingly.
- Confirm with all clues.

#### E. **Bird Perch Ordering with Mixed Clues**

- Use "to the left/right of" and fixed positions.
- Example: If owl is leftmost (position 1), quail is rightmost (position 5), cardinal is third from left (position 3), place others accordingly.
- Use "second from left/right" to fix positions.

---

### 8. **Tips for Avoiding Common Mistakes**

- Do not assume "older" means "to the left" unless explicitly stated.
- Carefully distinguish between "second-newest" (2nd place from newest) and "second-oldest" (2nd place from oldest).
- When multiple objects are compared indirectly, chain inequalities carefully.
- Confirm that all statements are satisfied in your final order.
- Use elimination on options by checking if the proposed answer contradicts any clue.

---

### 9. **Example Walkthrough (Antique Car Show)**

Given:

- Vehicles: convertible, sedan, tractor, minivan, limousine
- Tractor newer than minivan
- Tractor older than limousine
- Convertible older than sedan
- Convertible is second-newest

Step 1: Convertible is second-newest → position 4 (if newest is 5)

Step 2: Tractor newer than minivan → tractor > minivan

Step 3: Tractor older than limousine → limousine > tractor

Step 4: Convertible older than sedan → convertible < sedan

Step 5: Arrange: minivan < tractor < limousine

Step 6: Convertible is second-newest (4), so limousine is newest (5)

Step 7: Convertible < sedan → sedan > convertible (so sedan is newest or third-newest)

Step 8: Since limousine is newest, sedan must be older than convertible (contradiction?), so sedan is oldest.

Final order: minivan (oldest), tractor, limousine, convertible, sedan (newest)

Answer: minivan is oldest.
