**Cheat Sheet for Identifying Sarcasm in Challenging Examples**

---

### Key Indicators of Sarcasm

1. **Contradiction Between Description and Conclusion**
   - When a statement praises or defends something negative or criticizes something positive.
   - Example: Calling a lazy employee "the best" right after describing laziness.
   - Example: Saying "great transformation" when someone’s behavior worsens.

2. **Irony Through Opposite Meaning**
   - Saying the opposite of what is true or expected to highlight absurdity.
   - Example: Claiming "government control of the internet can't be bad" when it clearly can.
   - Example: "Pay-to-win content is more fun and accessible" when it’s widely disliked.

3. **Exaggeration or Absurdity**
   - Overstating or understating to an unrealistic degree to mock or ridicule.
   - Example: Complaining about a 2-minute wait as "too long."
   - Example: Suggesting banning breathing to mock banning vaping.

4. **Mocking or Ridiculing a Stereotype or Common Belief**
   - Using sarcasm to highlight the absurdity of stereotypes or false beliefs.
   - Example: "Women are totally incapable of pumping gas" to mock sexist stereotypes.
   - Example: "Muslims love celebrating Christmas" to mock misunderstanding of cultural practices.

5. **Using Positive Language to Describe Negative Situations (or Vice Versa)**
   - Praising something clearly bad or condemning something clearly good.
   - Example: Calling leaving space junk "brilliant."
   - Example: Calling genocide "the most rational decision."

6. **Rhetorical Questions or Statements That Pretend Ignorance**
   - Asking or stating something that implies the opposite is obvious.
   - Example: "Why would they have weapons drawn at a shooting?" (when it’s obvious).
   - Example: "No one is on the internet" (when obviously many are).

7. **Misapplication of Logic or Flawed Reasoning**
   - Presenting an obviously flawed argument as if it were sound.
   - Example: "If it hasn't happened before, it must not be illegal."
   - Example: "Only rich people can afford healthy food" (exaggerated to mock).

8. **Incongruous or Illogical Cause-Effect Claims**
   - Linking unrelated or absurd causes and effects.
   - Example: "Evolution causes vaccines."
   - Example: "Bought stock at $1050 and sold at $100. Such profits being made."

---

### Specific Challenging Patterns & How to Spot Them

| Pattern | How to Identify | Examples |
|---------|-----------------|----------|
| **Praise after Negative Description** | Look for positive adjectives or praise immediately following a negative or contradictory description. | "He's the best after showing laziness." "Brilliant person leaving space junk." |
| **Rhetorical Question with Obvious Answer** | The question implies the answer is obvious, but the speaker pretends not to know. | "Why have weapons drawn at a shooting?" |
| **Exaggerated Reaction to Minor Issue** | Complaints or reactions that are disproportionate to the situation. | "Two-minute wait is too long." "Dumpstering a $1000 phone." |
| **Mocking Stereotypes or Misconceptions** | Statements that exaggerate or invert stereotypes to highlight their absurdity. | "Women can't pump gas." "Muslims love Christmas." |
| **Flawed Logic Presented Seriously** | Statements that use faulty logic but are phrased as if they are reasonable. | "If it hasn't happened before, it must not be illegal." "Only rich people can afford healthy food." |
| **Opposite of Common Knowledge** | Statements that contradict well-known facts or principles. | "Ignorance of the law excuses you." "Diamonds are worthless." |
| **Sarcastic Use of "Perfectly Reasonable" or Similar Phrases** | Calling something obviously unreasonable "perfectly reasonable." | "$10 per mile fuel cost is perfectly reasonable." |
| **Using "So" or "Such" to Emphasize Irony** | Phrases like "Such profits being made" when losses occurred. | "Bought high, sold low. Such profits." |
| **Mocking Serious Issues with Flippant Tone** | Treating serious topics as trivial or praising negative outcomes. | "Genocide is the most rational decision." "Drinking every day, what a great transformation." |

---

### How to Apply This Cheat Sheet

1. **Check for Contradictions:**  
   Does the statement praise something negative or criticize something positive?  
   → Likely sarcasm.

2. **Look for Exaggeration or Absurdity:**  
   Is the reaction or claim wildly disproportionate or unrealistic?  
   → Likely sarcasm.

3. **Identify Irony or Opposite Meaning:**  
   Is the statement saying the opposite of what is commonly accepted or expected?  
   → Likely sarcasm.

4. **Evaluate Logic:**  
   Does the statement use flawed or absurd logic as if it were sound?  
   → Likely sarcasm.

5. **Consider Contextual Knowledge:**  
   Does the statement contradict well-known facts or social norms?  
   → Likely sarcasm.

6. **Watch for Rhetorical Questions or Mocking Tone:**  
   Is the speaker pretending ignorance or mocking a belief?  
   → Likely sarcasm.

### Examples of Difficult Cases and Why They Are Sarcastic

- **"He's a terrible person promoting a charity that raised millions."**  
  Contradiction between negative label and positive action.

- **"Government control of the internet can't be bad."**  
  Irony highlighting obvious negatives.

- **"Only rich people can afford healthy food."**  
  Exaggerated stereotype used to mock.

- **"Bought stock at $1050 and sold at $100. Such profits."**  
  Opposite of reality stated as fact.

- **"No one is on the internet."**  
  Contradicts obvious reality, mocking.

- **"Genocide is the most rational decision."**  
  Absurd praise of a horrific act.

- **"Two-minute wait is too long."**  
  Exaggerated complaint.

- **"Evolution causes vaccines."**  
  Illogical cause-effect claim.

Question: Which statement is sarcastic?
Options:
(A) He's just kindhearted. Okay, well nothing wrong with him running the country in that case!
(B) He's just demented. Okay, well nothing wrong with him running the country in that case!
Reasoning: Let's think step by step.  
We need to identify which statement is sarcastic. Option (A) says "He's just kindhearted. Okay, well nothing wrong with him running the country in that case!" — describing someone as "kindhearted" and then saying there's "nothing wrong" with them running the country is a sincere, logical statement. Option (B) says "He's just demented. Okay, well nothing wrong with him running the country in that case!" — calling someone "demented" (a serious negative trait) and then claiming there's "nothing wrong" with them running the country is a contradiction, since no one would sincerely believe that being "demented" is acceptable for a leader. This contradiction signals sarcasm. So the answer is (B).
Answer: (B)

Question: Which statement is sarcastic?
Options:
(A) Why would they have weapons drawn when they've been called to the scene of a fire?
(B) Why would they have weapons drawn when they've been called to the scene of a shooting?
Reasoning: Let's think step by step.  
We need to identify which statement is sarcastic. Option (A) asks why someone would have weapons drawn when responding to a fire — since there is no obvious threat at a fire, this is a sincere question expressing confusion. Option (B) asks the same question but about a shooting, where having weapons drawn would be expected and appropriate. Questioning why weapons would be drawn at a shooting is ironic, since the answer is obvious, making this statement sarcastic. So the answer is (B).
Answer: (B)
